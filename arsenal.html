10 — Reference

The Arsenal

Behind every alert in this dataset is a physical object: a pipe rocket welded in a Gaza workshop, a Soviet-designed Katyusha, a Shahed drone droning at 185 km/h, or a six-tonne ballistic missile arriving from Iran at two kilometres per second. This is a field guide to all of them — what they are, how far they fly, what they carry, and what it costs to shoot them down. A $500 Qassam can force a $50,000 interceptor shot; Iran's June 2025 barrage cost it an estimated $1–6 billion, and defending against it cost more.

The Duel

Fire one. Then fire eighty.

Pick a system and launch it at the deepest target it can reach — watch the trajectory, the interceptor rising to meet it, and the bill both sides run up. Then stack the salvo: interception rates that look unbeatable at ×1 start leaking at ×80, which is the whole logic of saturation barrages.

Illustrative simulation, not a model: intercept rates are anchored to publicly reported figures (~90%+ single-threat claims, ~86–90% across the June 2025 barrages), costs to the documented estimates above — * marks class-level cost estimates. Gaza — Hamas & PIJ

8 systems

Qassam family

Qassam-1/2/3 · al-Qassam rocket
Classartillery rocket
Range3–16 km
Warhead5–20 kg
Guidanceunguided
Est. cost / round$300–$800
First used vs Israel2001 (first launches; first deep hit on Sderot 2002)

Warhead: Improvised HE (scavenged TNT / urea-nitrate fertilizer mixes) packed with shrapnel; earliest Qassam-1 carried under 1 kg, typical later rounds 5-10 kg, Qassam-3 up to ~20 kg.

In action: October 7, 2023: 2,200-5,000 rockets (largely Qassam/Grad types plus mortars) fired from Gaza in the opening ~20-30 minutes — the largest rocket barrage ever fired at Israel.

The archetypal Gaza rocket: made in workshops from pipe steel and fertilizer, wildly inaccurate but launched in enormous numbers. Its sub-$1,000 cost against ~$50,000 Iron Dome interceptors is the classic cost-asymmetry example. Sources: [1] [2] [3]

122mm Grad

Katyusha · BM-21 Grad
Classartillery rocket
Range20–40 km
Warhead18–30 kg
Guidanceunguided (spin/fin stabilized)
First used vs IsraelKatyusha fire from Lebanon since the 1970s; Grad-type fire from Gaza documented from 2006-2008

Warhead: HE-fragmentation; standard rounds ~18-21 kg, extended-range and Hezbollah variants documented up to ~30 kg.

In action: 2006 Lebanon war: Hezbollah fired 3,970-4,228 rockets in 34 days, ~95% of them 122mm Katyushas. November 24, 2024: ~250 projectiles from Lebanon in one day. The workhorse of Gaza barrages incl. May 2021 (4,360 rockets in 11 days).

The single most-fired weapon family behind Israel's red-alert sirens across every front — Hamas, PIJ and Hezbollah all use the same Soviet-designed 122mm round. Sources: [1] [2] [3] [4]

Mortars (60/81/120mm)

mortar bombs
Classmortar
Range1–9 km
Warheadundisclosed
Guidanceunguided
First used vs IsraelEarly 2000s (Second Intifada, from Gaza)

Warhead: Standard HE-fragmentation mortar bombs; a 120mm bomb weighs ~13-16 kg with a few kg of explosive fill.

In action: October 7, 2023: IDF counted ~3,000 rockets and mortar bombs fired in the first four hours; mortars blanketed Gaza-envelope communities throughout the war.

The shortest-range threat in the dataset: too fast and low for many Iron Dome engagements close to the fence, mortars caused a large share of casualties in Gaza-border communities in 2014 and 2023. Sources: [1] [2]

The M-75 marked Hamas's leap from border-area rockets to holding metropolitan Tel Aviv at risk with an indigenized Iranian design. Hamas even released a perfume called 'M75' to celebrate it. Sources: [1] [2]

Iran's signature proxy rocket: the transfer of Fajr-5s to both Hezbollah and Gaza was the first big step in extending proxy reach from border towns to Israel's population centers. Sources: [1] [2]

Its 2014 debut forced alert coverage as far north as Haifa and showed Syrian-produced heavy rockets had reached Gaza by sea. Sources: [1] [2]

Longest-range weapon known in Hamas's arsenal, named for bombmaker Yahya Ayyash. Its 250 km claim puts essentially all of Israel inside Gaza rocket range on paper. Sources: [1] [2]

Badr-3

Badr 3
Classheavy artillery rocket
Range13–160 km
Warhead250–400 kg
Guidanceunguided
First used vs IsraelMay 5, 2019 (PIJ launches at the Ashkelon area)

Warhead: Very heavy HE warhead for a Gaza rocket — 250 kg of explosive per PIJ, 300-400 kg total per analyst Fabian Hinz; airbursts ~20 m up for wide fragmentation. Range claims disagree sharply: ~13 km as verified in use, 150-160 km claimed.

In action: Debuted during the ~700-rocket May 2019 escalation; fired again in the May 2021 war and 2022-23 Gaza rounds; IDF captured Badr-3 stocks inside Gaza in 2024.

PIJ's showcase Iranian-designed heavy rocket — what it lacks in range and accuracy it makes up in warhead mass, several times heavier than typical Palestinian rockets. Sources: [1] [2]

In action: Used repeatedly in multi-rocket strikes on IDF border posts through 2024; footage of Burkan impacts became a Hezbollah propaganda staple.

A weapon born of Syria's siege warfare adopted against Israeli border fortifications — trades range for one of the heaviest warheads of any rocket fired at Israel. Sources: [1]

Zelzal-2

Zilzal-2
Classheavy artillery rocket
Range~210 km
Warhead~600 kg
Guidanceunguided (spin-stabilized); Iranian kits convert it into a Fateh-110-class guided missile
First used vs IsraelNot documented as fired — held as a strategic stockpile (Israel destroyed Zelzal launchers on the ground in the 2006 war's opening strikes)

The heart of Hezbollah's 'precision project': from 2016 Iran smuggled GPS-guidance conversion kits for Zelzal-2s — a program Israel repeatedly bombed. Sources: [1] [2]

ClassSRBM

Warhead: 450-500 kg HE; the Qader-1 fired at Tel Aviv carried a 500 kg warhead. Precision-guided versions are the core of Hezbollah's PGM project — CEPs from ~500 m down to tens of meters claimed.

In action: Single-missile use documented (Sept 25, 2024). Israeli intelligence assessed Hezbollah held hundreds of Fateh-110/M-600-class missiles; most were targeted in the Sept–Nov 2024 air campaign.

The most strategically watched item in Hezbollah's arsenal: a genuinely guided ballistic missile family, its lone combat use against Tel Aviv intercepted. Sources: [1] [2]

Warhead: Up to ~40 kg of explosives in one-way attack configuration.

In action: October 13, 2024: two Mirsads launched from Lebanon; one evaded detection and hit a Golani Brigade dining hall in Binyamina — 4 soldiers killed, ~60 wounded, the deadliest single Hezbollah drone strike of the war.

Hezbollah's drone campaign proved harder to detect than rockets — low, slow and small, several crossed 40+ km into Israel before interception or impact. Sources: [1] [2]

Guidanceelectro-optical seeker with datalink — fire-and-observe, Spike-style lofted attack profile

Yemen — the Houthis

5 systems

Palestine-2

Filastin-2
ClassMRBM
Range1,450–2,150 km
Warhead~500 kg
Guidanceinertial/satnav with maneuvering re-entry vehicle (assessed)
First used vs IsraelSeptember 15, 2024 (first missile from Yemen to reach central Israel)

Warhead: ~500 kg claimed; two-stage solid fuel with a maneuvering re-entry vehicle. Houthi 'hypersonic' branding (claimed Mach 16) is treated with heavy skepticism by analysts — the Israeli Air Force said it has no evidence of a true hypersonic weapon.

In action: Fired singly, roughly weekly during peaks: 52 Houthi ballistic missiles + 13 drones at Israel between March 18 and June 28, 2025. May 4, 2025: one evaded THAAD and Arrow and hit Ben Gurion Airport's perimeter, injuring 6-8.

The Ben Gurion strike was the propaganda high-point of the Houthi campaign — a single missile that briefly halted international air traffic. Sources: [1] [2]

Zulfiqar (Houthi)

ClassMRBM
Range1,350–1,950 km
Warheadundisclosed
Guidanceinertial (Scud-lineage liquid-fuel MRBM)
First used vs IsraelOctober 31, 2023 (first Houthi ballistic missile attack on Israel — intercepted by Arrow 2, Arrow's first combat kill)

Warhead: Scud/Ghadr-lineage HE warhead, ~500-650 kg class assessed. Reaching Eilat from northern Yemen requires ~1,600+ km, which led analysts to conclude a Ghadr-class missile was in play.

In action: Single-missile launches typical of Houthi long-range strikes.

Together with Zulfiqar and the Palestine family, Toofan shows the Houthis fielding Iranian MRBM technology under local names — the first non-state actor to sustain 1,500+ km ballistic missile strikes. Sources: [1] [2]

The Tel Aviv hit exposed a gap in drone detection (the drone was tracked but not classified as hostile) and triggered Israel's first airstrike on Yemen the next day. Sources: [1] [2]

The Shield

What meets them in the air

Israel operates the most combat-tested layered air defense on earth — plus, since October 2024, a US Army THAAD battery. Every layer recorded world-first combat intercepts in this war.

Bottom layer

Iron Dome

InterceptsShort-range rockets, mortar bombs, and drones (threat ranges roughly 4-70 km); selective engagement — only shoots at projectiles headed for populated areas
Cost / interceptor$40k–$100k

~90%+ claimed interception rate since 2011, with 10,000+ combat intercepts by its 15th anniversary in April 2026; independent analysts estimate 80-90%, lower under saturation barrages. Sources: [1] [2]

Middle layer

David's Sling

InterceptsHeavy rockets, short-range ballistic missiles, cruise missiles and aircraft in the 40-300 km band — between Iron Dome and Arrow
Cost / interceptor$1M

First operational intercepts May 2023 (Gaza heavy rockets); intercepted Hezbollah's Qader-1 ballistic missile over Tel Aviv on Sept 25, 2024; engaged Iranian missiles 2024-2026. Sources: [1] [2]

Upper layer

Arrow 2

InterceptsBallistic missiles in the upper atmosphere / edge of space (terminal-phase)
Cost / interceptor$3M

First-ever combat intercept Oct 31, 2023 against a Houthi ballistic missile ~100 km up — described as the first combat interception in space; Arrow-family rates above 90% reported. Sources: [1]

Top layer

Arrow 3

InterceptsMedium/long-range ballistic missiles exoatmospherically (hit-to-kill in space) — Israel's top layer against Iranian MRBMs
Cost / interceptor$2M–$4M

First combat intercept Nov 9, 2023 (Houthi missile bound for Eilat); primary killer of Iranian MRBMs in the 2024, 2025 and 2026 barrages. Interceptor stocks ran low in June 2025, prompting a 2026 US-Israel co-production push. Sources: [1] [2]

Upper layer (allied)

THAAD (US Army)

InterceptsMedium/intermediate-range ballistic missiles in terminal high-altitude phase; US battery deployed to Israel October 2024
Cost / interceptor$12M–$13M

First-ever THAAD combat intercept Dec 27, 2024 vs a Houthi missile; at least six Houthi missiles downed by mid-2025; shared a publicized miss (with Arrow) on the May 4, 2025 Ben Gurion strike; heavily expended in the June 2025 war. Sources: [1]

Naval

C-Dome

InterceptsNaval Iron Dome on Sa'ar 6 corvettes — rockets, cruise missiles and drones approaching from the sea
Cost / interceptor$40k–$100k

First operational combat interception April 9, 2024 (hostile UAV approaching Eilat); protected the Eilat/Red Sea approach from Houthi drones and cruise missiles thereafter. Sources: [1]

Bottom layer (laser)

Iron Beam

InterceptsRockets, mortars and drones at short range using a ~100 kW-class high-power laser — unlimited magazine, complement to Iron Dome
Cost / interceptor~$2–5 / shot (electricity)

First operational system delivered to the Israeli Air Force Dec 28, 2025 — the world's first fielded high-power laser air-defense system; cost per shot cited at ~$2-5 of electricity vs $40k-100k per Iron Dome interceptor. Sources: [1] [2]

Stockpiles

How big are the arsenals?

Public estimates, with the caveat that every number here is contested and moves with each war.

Gaza — Hamas & PIJ

~20,000 rockets and mortar bombs on Oct 7, 2023 per Israeli estimates, most short-range. IDF one-year data: 13,200 projectiles fired from Gaza (≈5,000 on Oct 7 alone). Arsenal and production largely destroyed or depleted through 2024-25; fire fell to sporadic single launches and essentially stopped after the October 2025 Gaza ceasefire. Sources: [1] [2]

Lebanon — Hezbollah

Pre-Oct 2023: 120,000-200,000 rockets and missiles commonly cited (CSIS ~130,000; Israeli official estimate 150,000). IDF assessed 70-80% destroyed by the Nov 27, 2024 ceasefire. Early 2026: ~20,000-25,000 remaining; after the March-April 2026 Lebanon war, roughly 10,000-23,000 left, mostly short-range — still enough for 200-rocket barrage days. Sources: [1] [2]

Yemen — the Houthis

Total inventory not publicly quantified; capability is Iranian-resupplied. Launch record vs Israel: 200+ ballistic/cruise missiles and drones in the first 14 months; 52 ballistic missiles + 13 drones between Mar 18 and Jun 28, 2025; attacks paused after the Oct 2025 Gaza ceasefire and resumed Mar 28, 2026 for the Iran war. Sources: [1] [2]

Iran — direct

~2,500-3,000 ballistic missiles pre-June 2025 (US/Israeli estimates), of which ~2,000-2,500 could reach Israel. Fired ~550-590 at Israel in the June 2025 Twelve-Day War. Rebuilt toward ~2,500 Israel-range missiles by early 2026, then fired ~650 in the Feb-Apr 2026 war; an Israeli Air Force official estimated just over 1,000 remaining at the April 2026 ceasefire, with 300+ launchers destroyed cutting fire rates by 70-92%. Sources: [1] [2] [3]
